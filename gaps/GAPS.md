## 2. Strengths a skeptical reviewer would grant

1. **Mechanism shown in the machine code, per loop.** Packed SSE instructions NONE to FAST: reverse 6 to 0, stride-2 14 to 0, matrix multiply 22 to 0, one `ud2` added; forward loop identical (`audit/out-asm.txt`; remarks in each results folder; drawers on `site/index.html`). None of Google, NADER, NIST or Readyset shows the code for any case.
2. **Layout controlled, and a layout artefact caught.** A 1.6x "cost" on the forward add was traced to a 31-byte loop crossing a 64-byte boundary, then removed with an aligned pair and a layout A/A (`investigate/FINDINGS.md`, `harness/criteria-v2.json`). NADER explains its own checks-make-it-faster results by layout without testing it; Mytkowicz found no surveyed paper handled this. That is the project's most defensible methodological point.
3. **Controls and statistics that recompute.** Byte-identical A/A, layout A/A, negative and positive controls, NOVEC as a known slowdown, two-level bootstrap over jobs and repetitions, recomputed by separate code (`harness/pool_check.py`) and by `python scripts/check.py` with no compiler.
4. **A count nobody else has published.** 1 of 593 vectorized loops lost in 19 general libraries against 444 of 4,968 in 25 numeric projects, list committed before compiling (`corpus/PLAN2.md`), run twice byte-identical, 401 of 444 traced to a libc++ check (`corpus/README.md`). It is consistent with the sequel's "seven distinct places" of unsafe access: the expensive shape is rare in general code.
5. **Fixes that keep the checks, measured.** One up-front size check or `span::first(n)` brings reverse loops to 0.99 to 1.09x; row pointers for matrix multiply; and a trap found on the way (`std::transform` over `rbegin()` is fast because libc++ 18 FAST does not check vector iterators) (`mitigate/README.md`).
6. **A distinct GCC finding.** GCC 14 with `_GLIBCXX_ASSERTIONS` at -O3 loses SIMD even on the forward add (4.9 to 8.5x) (`siblings/README.md`); the search found no other public number for this.
7. **Honesty on record.** Day-one NO-GO kept; every rule file with its commit; a 124-claim audit with 24 overstated and 11 contradicted claims fixed (`claims-audit/CLAIMS.md`); "neither confirms nor contradicts Google" stated in the README's second paragraph.

## 3. The three attacks a hostile expert will make

**Attack 1: "Eight synthetic loops on SSE2 tell me nothing about 0.30%. Google measured a fleet built with PGO; you have no PGO and no timed real program."**
- What answers it: the README says the measurement is different and makes no claim on Google's number; the census shows how often the expensive shape occurs in real code; `realworld/` puts lost loops at about 0 to 6% of executed instructions (lines) in six programs, labelled not timed; `siblings/` covers x86-64-v3 and clang 20 thinly.
- What fails: no PGO build anywhere, though the sequel calls PGO critical; no real program in the pooled runs; json/fmt bounds of 0.2 to 3.3% cannot resolve 0.30%; the main toolchain is 2024 clang at baseline SSE2, and at x86-64-v3 matrix multiply stays vectorized (the AVX2 kernel control failed as registered). Gaps 3, 4, 7.

**Attack 2: "You moved the goalposts. The day-one test said NO-GO, you loosened the rule, added an 'aligned' column, then started excluding 'placement-sensitive' cells. EPYC 9V45's verdict rests on an interval ending at 0.9996."**
- What answers it: every rule file is committed before the runs it judges and kept (`dayone/criteria.json`, `harness/criteria.json`, `-v2`, `-v3`); v2 and v3 changed no threshold; the 9V45 fragility and the 32 of 192 out-of-interval cells on EPYC 7763 are printed in DETAILS.
- What fails: no run on fresh data judged by frozen rules; the exclusion rule itself was designed after seeing which cells failed; layout is sampled at two points, so "placement-sensitive" is a label, not a distribution (Stabilizer's objection). Gaps 5, 6.

**Attack 3: "Shared cloud VMs with no cycle counters, a CPU lottery, one Intel job. Your 64-byte-window story is a guess, and 2.8 to 8x is a range so wide it is not a measurement."**
- What answers it: A/A half-widths per CPU model (0.10% on EPYC 7763 with 19 jobs) are smaller than every lost-loop effect by two orders of magnitude; Cachegrind instruction ratios are identical across CPUs (reverse 7.9 to 8.0x), so the per-CPU spread in time is real hardware difference, not noise; the investigation tested the boundary hypothesis by forcing interleave 1 in NONE at a non-crossing offset (as fast as aligned FAST).
- What fails: the hardware mechanism is still inferred (stated as such in FINDINGS); Intel gets no verdict; no bare metal; no arm64 timing. Gaps 11, 12.
